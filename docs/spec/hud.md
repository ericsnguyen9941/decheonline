# In-game screen: panels, interface art, cursors, selection marker

Read from aoe1-decomp `src/scr_game.cpp`, `src/tpnl_obj.cpp`, `src/tpnl_inv.cpp`, `src/stat_obj.cpp`, `src/view.cpp`, `src/vw_main.cpp`, `src/player.cpp`, `src/sprite.cpp`, `src/Mouseptr.cpp`. Code: `apps/client/src/hud/`, selection and map drawing in `apps/client/src/units/unit-layer.ts`.

## Interface SLPs (interfac.drs ids; `data2/Interfac.drs` overrides `data/`)
The ids are `base + culture` (Egyptian 0, Greek 1, Babylonian 2, East Asian 3; Roman uses 53001-53012 from data2, not wired).
- Screen: `game_a/b/c` = 50733 / 50737 / 50741 (+culture) for 640x480 / 800x600 / 1024x768 (scr_game.cpp:2263-2277). Frame 0 is the top strip (20 px), frame 1 the bottom panel (126 px). The bottom panel's minimap spot is a magenta diamond: the game draws the minimap over it.
- Command buttons: `btncmd` 50721 (command icons), `btnunit` 50730 (unit icons 50x50, player colour), `btntech` 50729, `btnbldg_<culture>` 50704+culture (building icons), borders 50713+c (54x54 slot), 50717+c (72x20), 50747+c (108x20), `button_other` 50725+c.
- Object panel: `health` 50745 (26 frames 50x7, frame = 26 - 26 * hp / max), `itemicon` 50731 (carried resource icons).
- Rollover text: when the hovered object changes, `RGE_View` (view.cpp:2380-2410) calls `get_mouse_pointer_action_vars` and puts `get_string(text_id)` on the view's message panel (when the `rollover` option is on, the default), and removes it when the pointer leaves the object. With the attack cursor `text_id` is the table's `enemy_text_id`: 3701 "Click to attack this unit." for most groups, 3702 (building) for group 3, 3703 (wall) for 27, 3704 (boat) for 2 and 20-22. The port shows it for the attack cursor only, on `hud-message` (`text.ts` `attackRollover`, `UnitLayerOptions.onRollover`).
- `mcursors` 51000: frame 0 arrow, 1 hourglass, 2 move/target arrows, 3 hand (work, repair), 4 sword (attack), 5 ring (ground target), 6 help. `TRIBE_Player::get_mouse_pointer_action_vars` picks the facet from the click tables: 0 for an own unit or nothing to do, 3 for work targets, 4 attack, 5 convert/heal. A player's own units get facet 0 (the arrow). The table row is the target's `object_group`, the column its owner's diplomacy; both click tables (`Tribe1btn`/`Tribe2btnMouseRightClickTable`, src/game_globals.cpp:696, :762) give the same actions. Enemy action 1 or 8 shows the sword when the selection can attack (`sel_object_properties & 1`: own groups whose `tribe_group_properties` (src/tplayer.cpp:176) has bit 1, plus towers 69/79/199/278) and the target's `object_state` is at most active; groups 11 (missiles, dead units, the Flare) and the resource groups have none. Port: `apps/client/src/units/mouse-actions.ts`; a right click there sends `attack`.
- Menu backgrounds 50100-50153, dialog frames etc. are imported (source `interface` in the pack) but not used yet. Palette 50500 is a `bina`.

## Layout (`TRIBE_Screen_Game::handle_size`, scr_game.cpp:757)
The original runs at a fixed size (>= 1024x768, >= 800x600, else 640x480 picks the art); screen coordinates at 1024x768:
top strip 0..20: resource text at x 32 / 99 / 166 / 233 (wood, food, gold, stone; the icons are in the art), y 2; age text at (270, 2, 492x16); chat (772, 0, 72x19), diplomacy (844, 0, 108x19), menu (952, 0, 72x19).
The age text is `get_string2(RGE_STRING_TYPE_AGE, attributes[6])` (scr_game.cpp:2351, tribegam.cpp:778): 4201 Stone, 4202 Tool, 4203 Bronze, 4204 Iron Age for ages 1-4; the sim's attribute 6 is 1 from the Stone Age start tech. The three buttons' texts are `get_string2(RGE_STRING_TYPE_COMMAND, id)` for `TRIBE_UI_COMMAND_CHAT/DIPLOMACY/MENU` (scr_game.cpp:252-258): 4113 Chat, 4114 Diplomacy, 4115 Menu. Chat opens the Send Messages dialog, and Enter the chat bar (`hud/chat.ts`, docs/spec/chat.md). Diplomacy opens the Diplomacy dialog (`command_diplomacy`, scr_game.cpp:3855; no hotkey; `hud/diplomacy.ts`, docs/spec/diplomacy.md §7), and another player's stance or tribute towards this one shows on the chat lines, the tribute with tribute.wav (diplomacy.md §7.4). Menu opens the sound volume part of Game Settings (`hud/options.ts`, docs/spec/sounds.md §5); the buttons click with button2.wav. Without strings these labels are empty; the HUD code holds no English for them (`text.test.ts` checks it). Bottom panel from y 642: object panel (5, 649, 127x114), 12 command slots 54x54 from (136, 650), 58 px between rows, minimap (795, 648, 220x114), help and score buttons (989, 732) and (989, 650), 30x30. 800x600 and 640x480 differ in the right-hand x positions (`layout.ts` has them); at 640x480 slots 5 and 11 are the wide "more"/"cancel" buttons.
Text colours for the Egyptian, Greek styles: black with a white shadow at (-1, +1) (`set_text_color(0, 0xFFFFFF)`); Babylonian, East Asian, Roman: white.

## Object panel (`TRIBE_Panel_Object::draw`)
From the panel corner: owner (civilization) name at (3, -2), object name at (3, 13), icon at (3, 30) (`master.button_pict` frame; `btnbldg` + building facet for buildings), hit point bar at (3, 84), `hp/max` text at (3, 92), then attack/armor rows. Text is white.

Stat rows (tpnl_obj.cpp:256-300, `draw_item`): each row is an `itemicon` 50731 frame at (54, 30 + 20 * row) and its value at (88, 33 + 20 * row); a value is `ValueTypeBonus`, the dat's displayed value (`orig_*`) and `+bonus` when the current one is higher. By `unit_level` (the dat's `interfaceKind`; 2 building, 3 villager, 4 soldier, 6 ranged/priest): attack (frame 7; levels 3, 4; current = the highest attack amount), armour (8; when above 0, always for 4 and 6; current = `base_armor` or a higher class-4 armour), pierce armour (10; above 0; class-3 armours), range (6; levels 4 and 6, current range > 0.99). A building (level 2) shows attack and range when above 0, unless it is unbuilt, or the player's own and training: then the progress row of the next section comes first. The Villager shows only `3` (attack). Not drawn yet: level 6's percent row, levels 5, 7 and 9's own rows, a building's held resource row (tpnl_obj.cpp:488-540). `apps/client/src/hud/text.ts` `statRows`.

Held resource row (tpnl_obj.cpp:364-424), after the stat rows, test id `object-held`: for the player's own unit of level 3, 4 or 6 when `(int)(attribute_amount_held + 0.5) >= 1` (`g_tactical_panel_rounding_offset` is -0.5), and for a level 1 object (trees, mines, forage, animals: Gaia's) whoever owns it, as row 0. Icon by `attribute_type_held`: food (0, 15, 16, 17) frame 2, wood (1) 0, stone (2) 1, gold (3) 3, trade goods (9) 5; another type draws no row. The value is `(int)(amount + 0.5)`. Another player's unit shows its stat rows but never its load (`owner != player` ends the panel). `game-hud.ts` `heldRow`.

## Selection (`RGE_Main_View::mouse_left_up_action`, vw_main.cpp:428-600; `RGE_Player::select_*`, player.cpp:1310-1460)
- The player has one `sel_list` of up to 25 objects and one `selected_obj`. `select_one_object` adds an object and makes it `selected_obj`, so it is the last one selected; `unselect_one_object` of `selected_obj` falls back to the first left in the list.
- The object panel (`TRIBE_Panel_Object`) shows `selected_obj` only, whatever the selection holds (scr_game.cpp:1183 `object_panel->set_object(selected_obj)`; the panel has a single `game_obj`). AoE1 has no panel of icons for a multi-selection; a multi-selection shows in the grid as Group and Ungroup (below) and on the map as one diamond and HP bar each. `GameHud` reads `HudSelection.primary` (`UnitLayer.primary`).
- Left click, two-button mouse (`interface_style` 2; right click gives orders): the topmost object under the pointer whose master's `select_level` (the dat's interaction mode, pack `PackUnit.selectLevel`) is 2 or more: own units (4), buildings (3), trees, mines, forage and animals (2), another player's objects too. On an object already selected with others, it alone; on another, it alone with its group (`select_object`); on nothing, the selection is emptied. A second click on the same spot passes over `selected_obj` to the next object under it. Shift or Ctrl toggles the object; adding first empties the selection when `selected_obj` is another player's or the selection's lowest `select_level` differs from the object's. Fog of war (the `get_visible` test) is not ported.
- Picking (`RGE_Main_View::pick1`, vw_main.cpp:983; `RGE_View::pick_objects`, view.cpp:3125; `sprite_check`, view.cpp:3306; `TRIBE_Main_View::pick_weight`, tvw_main.cpp:601). The click is `pick_objects(x, y, 10, 20, 15, 4, 1)`: every drawn SLP part of draw level 10 to 20 (each draw-list part is its own node with its own frame) gives its object a confidence, the best of its parts: 1 on a drawn pixel, 2 inside the row's outline span (from the row's first drawn pixel to one past its last, see-through gaps included; an empty row has none), 3 inside the frame box (`x2 = x + width - 1`), 4 within 15 px of it. `pick_weight` by the master's `select_level` (towers 69, 79, 199, 278 count as 3): level 4 gives 4 for confidence 1-2, 3 for 3, 2 for 4; level 3 gives 3 for 1-2, 2 for 3 on a foundation (`object_state` below active), else 0; level 2 gives 3 for 1-2, 2 for 3, else 0; other levels 1. The heaviest object wins; on a tie the first in the pick list, which runs by confidence, then higher draw level, then (ours) the sprite drawn on top. So a building is picked anywhere inside its drawn rows, the grass between the Stone Age Town Center's tents included, but not in the empty corners of its frame; a unit also in its frame box and up to 15 px round it, and over a building there. Right-click targets (`pick_best_target`, vw_main.cpp:1117) use the same picks and weights; ours takes the heaviest object the order table accepts. The dat graphic's own select box (`box_x1..y2`, set on a few fish and Tree13 only) and the `UC_ObjectTouched` weight 5 are not ported. Port: `apps/client/src/units/pick.ts`, `UnitLayer.topmostAt`; `composite.ts` keeps each baked part's frame for it.
- Box: `pick_multi(4, ...)` (view.cpp:1299, 3982) selects the player's own objects of `select_level` 4 in the box, up to 25. Its fallback (no unit in the box: the best object near the box centre within 50 px) is not ported.
- Another player's or Gaia's object, selected, shows its panel (owner line, name, hit points, stat rows) and its HP bar over the diamond; the grid has only Unselect (`setup_buttons`: `game_obj->owner != player`).
- Groups (`group_objects(-1)`, player.cpp:1452): the Group button gives the selected own objects a new `selected_group` from 11 up; clicking one of them selects the whole group (`select_object`). Ungroup (`ungroup_objects`) clears it for the selected objects. The groups live in the client, as in the original (no command is sent). Numbered groups 1-10 are the same `selected_group` field, so an object is in one group only (`apps/client/src/units/group-keys.ts`, `UnitLayer.assignGroup` / `recallGroup`). `TRIBE_Screen_Game::key_down_action` (src/scr_game.cpp) reads the virtual keys '0'-'9' (the digit row; digit 0 is group 10, `command_group_by_number` :3961, `command_select_group` :4016) and takes the first modifier held, Ctrl, then Shift, then Alt:
  - Ctrl+digit (:1466): `group_objects(id)` (player.cpp:1454): every object in group `id` leaves it, then the selected own objects join it (replacing a group they were in). With nothing selected nothing happens.
  - digit (:1778-1793): `command_select_group(id, 1)`: `unselect_object`, then `select_group` (player.cpp:1527) adds the player's objects in the group, in object list order, the first with its selection sound; `selected_obj` is the last. An empty group leaves the selection empty. A group of 11 and up is not brought in whole by this: only a click does (`select_object`, group > 0x0A).
  - Shift+digit (:1470-1485): the same without `unselect_object`, so the group is added to the selection.
  - Alt+digit (:1525-1543): as the bare digit, then `command_view_selected` (:4298) scrolls the view to `selected_obj`'s position. There is no double-tap: the original centres only with Alt.
  - Dead objects: `die_die_die` and `destroy_obj` (stat_obj.cpp:1843-1858) unselect the object, and `removeObject` drops it from the player's list, which `select_group` walks, so it is gone from its group. The port drops an object from the selection and its group when it starts dying (`ObjectState.Dying`).
  - The group's number: a selected own object in group 1-9 draws frame `selected_group` of groupnum.shp (interfac.drs 50403, `get_shape(0)`) at the diamond's fourth corner (`-radius_x, -radius_y`), 6 px left and 8 up, at draw level 30 (stat_obj.cpp:707). Group 10 and the Group button's groups (11 and up) draw none. `UnitLayer.drawGroupNumber`.
  - Group ids of the Group button (`group_objects(-1)`, player.cpp:1454): the first id from 11 up that `groups_used` does not hold, which then holds it for good (ungrouping frees nothing), up to 126. At 127 the used set is cleared and rebuilt from the player's objects' `selected_group` (numbered groups included), and the first free id from 1 up is taken (so a wrapped group can be 1-10: its number is then drawn, its digit recalls it, and a click no longer selects it whole); with all 126 in use nothing happens. With nothing selected the button does nothing. `UnitLayer.newGroupId`.
  - The selection order is `sel_list` (player.cpp:1337, `unselect_one_object` :1424): new objects take the first free slot, and a leaver is replaced by the last object (swap-remove), so after one is deselected the order differs from the order of selection; `selected_obj` goes to the first slot when it was the leaver. Tab walks this order (`UnitLayer.deselect`).
  - Not ported: the browser's own use of Ctrl+digit and Alt+digit (tab switching) which a page cannot always cancel.

## Hotkeys
The letters are language strings, read as the first character, an upper case virtual key (`TRIBE_Screen_Game::TRIBE_Screen_Game` scr_game.cpp:122-129, `set_button` :3085). language.dll / languagex.dll (English, from the pack's `manifest.strings`; the client reads them, never hard-codes a letter) give: Build B (0x1037), Stop S (0x1038), Group G (0x1039), Ungroup U (0x103A), More X (0x103B), Stand Ground D (0x103D), Attack Ground T (0x103E), view Town Center H (0x103F), the eight building keys N B D A K L P Y (0x1040-0x1047), Heal E, Convert C, Move M (0x104A), Work W (0x104B), Attack A (0x104C), Repair R (0x104D), Unload L (0x104E). `key_down_action` (:1451-1780) takes them in this order (`units/select-keys.ts`, `hud/hotkeys.ts`, `hud/command-panel.ts`):
- Shift+digit, Shift+F2-F7 (formations, not ported), Shift+Tab (:1472-1523, Tab :1521): `command_tab_selected(0)`.
- Alt (:1529): digits only (Alt+digit above); a letter with Alt does nothing.
- Without Ctrl, in the build menu (`game_mode` build menu, `sub_game_mode` 1, :1586): the hotkey of any building of the list (the other page's too) plays the button click and runs `command_building` (placing starts when the player can pay, else the refusal message).
- Ctrl+hotkey `i` of the eight (:1605): `command_select_building(i)` (:4177) looks for the player's building of master 0x6D Town Center, 0x0C Barracks, 0x2D Dock, 0x57 Archery Range, 0x31 Siege Workshop, 0x65 Stable, 0x68 Temple, 0 Academy (keys N B D A K L P Y): `find_obj` (tplayer.cpp:627) takes the next one in the object list after `selected_obj` when that is of the same master (one under construction counts there), wrapping to the first finished one (`object_state` Active); with one found, `unselect_object`, `select_object` (with its sound), `set_view_loc` to it; none: nothing changes. `UnitLayer.selectBuilding`.
- The view Town Center key H (:1616), with or without Ctrl: `command_select_building(0)`, the same as Ctrl+N.
- Without Ctrl, outside the build menu (:1757): Work (W) and Attack (A) are 0 in the constructor (`work_hotkey`, `attack_hotkey`), so bind nothing; Move (M) `command_move` (:4060): with an own active object selected, the message "Click an area to move there." (3040, 0x0BE0) shows and the mode `RGE_GAME_MODE_COMMAND_MOVE` is set; the next left click on the map is `command_make_move` (vw_main.cpp:1231): the selection walks there and the mode ends (a click off the map only ends it). The port leaves the mode with that click or when the selection is emptied (Esc on units is Unselect); it keeps the normal cursor. Ungroup (U): `command_ungroup` even when no Ungroup button shows.
- Tab (:1796, `command_tab_selected(1)` :4376): with two or more selected, `selected_obj` becomes the next object of `sel_list` (the first after the last), Shift+Tab the previous (the last before the first). The selection itself does not change; the panel shows the new object. `tabTarget`.
- The grid buttons (`TButtonPanel::handle_key_down`, pnl_btn.cpp:759): a visible, enabled button whose `hotkey` is the key, with no Alt or Ctrl (Shift is ignored: `hotkey_shift` 0), once per press (no key repeat), plays its click sound and acts as a left click. A button's key (`set_button`): Build, Stop, Group, Ungroup, More, Stand Ground, Repair by their `STRING_ID_HOTKEY_*`; Cancel and Unselect Esc; a train or build item its master's `hotkey_id` string (the Villager 16132 "C", a Town Center "N" ...: pack version 10 `PackUnit.hotkeyId`); research (the dat's tech `hotkey_id` is 0 for every tech) and Cancel Construction (the call passes 0) have none. The key acts through the same code as the click (`CommandPanel.run`), so a button the grid does not show does nothing. The first matching button in slot order wins.
- Stop (S) for buildings and units, Repair (R, `command_repair`: arms the repair click, docs/spec/commands.md §4) and Stand Ground (D) act (commands.md). Inert, no button (the sim has no such units, commands.md §5): Attack Ground, Heal, Convert, Unload.
- Not ported: Shift+F2-F7 formations, F4 score, F5 fps, Ctrl+key developer commands, the cursor of the Move mode, a double-tap or the idle-villager key (the key handler has none).

## Command buttons (`TRIBE_Screen_Game::setup_buttons`, scr_game.cpp:2409; `do_button_action`)
- Grid: 12 slots, 0-4 the top row, 6-10 the bottom row; slot 5 is "more" and slot 11 is cancel/unselect (hotkey Esc). Each icon is a 50x50 frame inside the 2 px slot border (border SLP 50713 + culture, `TRIBE_Panel_Button::draw` type 0).
- Slot of a master: `calc_button_loc` maps the dat `button_location` 1-5 to slots 0-4, 6-10 to 6-10 (first page), 11-15 to 0-4 and 16-20 to 6-10 (second page). Without a Range (87) among the buildable items, the Town Center's button moves to location 6. A later button set in the same slot replaces the earlier one.
- Items: `interface_item_avail` lists the player's enabled masters (master type >= 70) whose `train_location` is the maker: the Builder (the build menu) or the building's id. A player without a Town Center may build one while attribute 48 is 0.
- Icons: `btncmd` 50721 (data2 frames are 800x600 sheets with the icon top left): Build 2, Cancel Construction 3, Cancel and Unselect 10, More 11. Building items: `btnbldg` 50704 + culture, frame `icon + buildingFacet` (the facet the age gives). Unit items: `btnunit` 50730 frame `icon`, player colour.
- Modes (`game_mode`): normal; build menu (`TRIBE_GAME_MODE_BUILD_MENU`) with the items, "more" when any item is on the other page, and Cancel; placing a building with only Cancel.
- The branch is `unit_level_selected()`: the level every selected object shares, 0 when they differ (the pack's `level`; without a pack, guessed: buildings 2, builders 3, other units 4).
- Level 3 (Villagers) get Build in slot 0, Repair (btncmd frame 0, text 4127) in slot 1, Stop (frame 3) in slot 2 while a selected object has an action (`any_selected_have_action`), and Unselect. Level 4 (soldiers) get Stand Ground (frame 4, text 4122) in slot 0 and Stop in slot 1; levels 5, 7 and 9 Stop in slot 0, level 6 in slot 2; mixed levels Stop in slot 1 (with Build) or 0 (`setup_buttons`, scr_game.cpp:2496-2960). The level 5 trade buttons are not ported. Stop sends `stop` for the selected objects of select level 3 or more; Stand Ground `stand-ground` for those of level 4. Repair arms the repair mode (message 3052 "Click the building or boat to repair."); its next left or right click sends `repair` for the selected Villagers when `command_make_repair` accepts the picked object (own building, short of hit points, not dying), flashing it, else plays cantdo.wav (`UnitLayer.armRepair`). The original's game mode turns the grid to Cancel only while it is armed and Esc only leaves the mode; ours keeps the grid and Esc unselects (not ported).
- Group and Ungroup: with more than one object selected, Group (btncmd frame 7) in slot 6 and Ungroup (frame 8) in slot 7; with one that is in a group, Ungroup in slot 6. Levels 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9 always get them, a building only when it has nothing to train or research (`make_cnt == 0 && tech_cnt == 0`), a foundation never. Mixed levels (0) and other levels get Build when a selected object is of object group 4 (`is_group_selected(4)`), Group/Ungroup, and in the last slot Unselect when every one is a unit of level 3-9, else Cancel (`command_cancel` unselects in the normal mode). A foundation of one's own gets Cancel Construction in slot 0. An active building gets its train items: with a queue only the queued items stay, each with its count over the icon; left click sends `queue` +1, right click `queue` -1 (takes one off the stack, refunding it). Another player's object gets only Unselect.
- Research and Stop on an active own building (scr_game.cpp:2731-2880): `work_status` first. A research short of 100% (`TRIBE_Action_Make_Tech`, work type 103) leaves only Stop (btncmd frame 3) in slot 0 and Unselect. Otherwise, with a production queue, Stop goes in slot 6 and only the queued train items show (no techs, no grouping). Without a queue the train items come first, then `interface_tech_avail`: the player's techs in state 1 (available) whose `location` is the building's id, in tech id order, each at `calc_button_loc(tech.button_location)`. A Town Center with no tech to offer and age (attribute 6) below 4 shows tech `age + 100` (the next age) disabled, unless that tech is disabled (-1): its picture draws every other pixel (`fog_next_shape`, shadowing mask 0xFF00FF; the client masks the icon with a 2x2 checkerboard) and the button takes no mouse (`set_disabled`). Grouping shows only with nothing to train or research. Research icons: `btntech` 50729, frame `tech.icon`, in the player's colour, inside border frame 2 (pressed 3) instead of 0/1 (`border_frame = 2` for `TRIBE_UI_COMMAND_RESEARCH`). The message is `calc_text_msg(tech.string_id2, tech_cost)`: "Advance to Tool Age: Requires 2 Stone Age buildings (Food: 500)" (8063); `tech_cost` lists each used slot, and with gold replacement (attribute 29 >= 1) moves the shortfall to the first gold or empty slot. Stop's message is its command text, 4105 "Stop (S)". A click on a live research button runs `command_research`: `check_tech_cost` passes, then the selected building gets the `research` command; else the attribute short gives the refusal (3001 ...). Stop sends `stop` for the building (research and queue refunded by the sim). Stop for units: see the level 3 and 4 lines above. Whether a disabled button still puts its message on the line is not settled (unverified); the client shows it.
- Research progress: the object panel's item row (frame 9) shows `get_progress`, `trunc(research_done * 100 / research_time)` (100 once the action is done), ahead of the training row; an own researching building hides its stat rows like a training one.

## Elevation (`RGE_Map::get_tile_type`, src/map.cpp:2268; `coordinate_map`, :393; `RGE_View::draw_tile`, src/view.cpp:2845)
Random maps (docs/spec/random-maps.md §5.4) give every tile a height level 0-8 (`RGE_Tile::height`, `TileMap.elevation`). The port: `apps/client/src/map/elevation.ts` (`Ground`), used by `map-view.ts`, `units/unit-layer.ts`, `hud/minimap.ts`, `map/debug.ts`.
- Tile type (`get_tile_type`, map.cpp:2268, recomputed for a tile and its 8 neighbours whenever a height changes, map.cpp:2125): from which neighbours are exactly one level higher, a slope shape 0-16. In map terms (the decomp's n/e/w/s/nw/sw/ne/se): n = row - 1, e = col + 1, w = col - 1, s = row + 1, nw = (row - 1, col + 1), sw = (row + 1, col - 1), ne = (row - 1, col - 1), se = (row + 1, col + 1); an off-map neighbour counts as level. First match wins: n and e 14; w and s 13; n and w 16; e and s 15; n 6; e 8; w 5; s 7; nw 10 (2 if w and s are both one lower); sw 9 (1 if n and e are one lower); ne 11 (3 if e and s are one lower); se 12 (4 if n and w are one lower); else 0. Seen on the screen each type raises some of the diamond's corners by one level (left = map corner (0, 0) of the tile, top (1, 0), bottom (0, 1), right (1, 1)): 1 and 9 bottom; 2 and 10 top; 3 and 11 left; 4 and 12 right; 5 left+bottom; 6 left+top; 7 bottom+right; 8 top+right; 13 left+bottom+right; 14 left+top+right; 15 top+bottom+right; 16 left+top+bottom. Each half of the tile is planar: types 3, 4, 9, 10, 15, 16 fold along the left-right diagonal, the others along the top-bottom one (read off `teleport`'s height table, below; 1-4 are 9-12 folded the other way).
- Box (`init_tile_sizes`, map.cpp:175; `elev_height` 16 from the dat's terrain block, equal to `tile_half_height`): `tilesizes[type].height` is 32 for 0, 3, 4, 11, 12, 15, 16; 16 (`elev_height`) for 1, 5, 7, 9, 13; 48 (`elev_height + tile_height`) for 2, 6, 8, 10, 14. `coordinate_map` (map.cpp:425-438): `screen_xpos = (row + col) * 32`, `screen_ypos = (row - col) * 16 - height * 16`, and 16 more up for the types with the top corner raised (2, 6, 8, 10, 14, 15, 16). The tile's frame is drawn with its top-left (hotspot 0, 0 in every terrain SLP) at that box; the view culls on the same box (view.cpp:1656-1677).
- Picture (`get_tile_picture`, view.cpp:3535): the terrain's `tiles[type]` (`RGE_TOB_Picts`, the dat's 19 `elevationGraphics` `[frameCount, angleCount, shapeId]`): frame `shapeId + angleCount * variant`, the variant `(row % rows) * cols + (col % cols)` only when `frameCount` > 1, else 0. Grass (15001) has 9 flat variants then one frame per type 1-16 at 9-24 (65x17, 65x49, 63x33, ...); water (15002) 4 variants then 4-19. The slope frames are drawn shaded in the art (no shading at run time). Pack version 12 carries `PackTerrain.tiles`.
- Draw order (`RGE_View::draw`, view.cpp:1586-1600): the view scans screen rows top to bottom, each row left to right, so a raised tile is drawn over the boxes of the tiles behind it (`Ground.rank`). A flat map keeps the port's old row order (no overlaps there). Objects of level 6-40 are drawn after all the terrain (fog-of-war section), so a hill never hides a unit behind it.
- Objects (`RGE_Static_Object::teleport`, stat_obj.cpp:1495-1650): `world_z` of a ground object (`movement_type` 0) is the tile's height plus `rz`, the slope's height at the fraction (rx, ry) of the tile (types 5 `1 - rx`, 6 `1 - ry`, 7 `ry`, 8 `rx`, 9 `max(ry - rx, 0)`, 10 `max(rx - ry, 0)`, 11 `max(1 - rx - ry, 0)`, 12 `max(rx + ry - 1, 0)`, 13 `rx + ry < 1 ? 1 - rx : ry`, 14 `rx + ry < 1 ? 1 - ry : rx`, 15 `max(rx, ry)`, 16 `rx <= ry ? 1 - rx : 1 - ry`, 1 `rx + ry < 1 ? ry : 1 - rx`, 2 `rx + ry < 1 ? rx : 1 - ry`, 3 `rx <= ry ? 1 - ry : 1 - rx`, 4 `rx <= ry ? rx : ry`); a flying one (`movement_type` 1) keeps its own `world_z` while above that. `screen_y_offset = (zd + ry + 1 - rx - world_z) * 16`, `zd` 1 for the raised-top types, and the view draws a tile's objects at `screen_ypos + height * tile_half_height` (view.cpp:2179), so an object is drawn at its flat map pixel (`get_point`, map.cpp:2584) less `world_z * 16`. The shadow offset (draw level 10 or less, "Missile shadows") uses the ground's `rz` instead of `world_z`. The sim sets `world_z` this way (`TileMap.groundZ`, f32 as the original, movement.md §4) and the client's `Ground.z` calls that same function, so a ground object's sim `z` is exactly the height `Ground.pixel` draws it at, also at the in-between points the client slides a unit through; a flying object (a missile) is drawn higher by its sim `z` less the ground's height there, its shadow parts on the ground. The selection diamond and the building being placed are drawn at the ground's height.
- Centring the view (`RGE_Player::set_view_loc`, src/player.cpp:993, keeps the point on the map; `RGE_View::update`, src/view.cpp:1061-1093): `map_scr_y_offset = screen_ypos + height * elev_hgt - center_scr_row` of the centre tile, and the point's fraction in the tile is turned into whole pixels with truncation (`g_view_tile_fraction_scale` 0.5). Adding the height back cancels the tile's raise, so the view centres on the point's flat place, not where it is drawn on a hill; only `coordinate_map`'s 16 px for a raised top corner (types 2, 6, 8, 10, 14, 15, 16) stays in `screen_ypos`, so the view sits 16 px higher on such a centre tile. The port: `Ground.viewPixel`, `MapView.lookAt`, used by every centring (game start on the Town Center, centring on the selection, the building and idle keys, the minimap click, `?debug=combat`, `window.__map.centerOnTile`). Not ported: where on the screen that point lands (the original puts it at `pnl_wid / 2 - tile_half_wid` plus the fraction, not the panel's exact middle; the port keeps the camera's centre). The minimap's view frame still reads the centre back as a flat pixel (`minimap.ts` `drawViewRect`), so it can be one tile off on a raised-top centre tile.
- Picking a map point (`view_function` mode 0x28, view.cpp:1781-1800): every tile in draw order whose box holds the mouse and whose frame pixel there is opaque (`hit_tile`, `shape_check`, view.cpp:3624) replaces the pick, so the front-most drawn tile wins; `tile_map_coords` (map.cpp:1880) turns the pixel inside the box into the fraction of the tile on its slope. The port tests each tile's two planar halves as drawn (`Ground.pointAt`), which gives the same tile and the point on its surface; a pixel off every tile reads flat (and may be off the map, as before). Right-click orders, the Move key, building placement and `window.__map.tileAt` use it.
- Picking an object: the port tests the sprite where it is drawn (`unit-layer.ts` `topmostAt`), so a unit on a hill is hit where it shows. Unverified: the decomp's `hit_object` (view.cpp:1754) gets the row's flat `start_screen_y` while the draw gets the raised one, which would put the hit box of an object on a raised-top tile 16 px under its picture; not ported.
- Fog and the map edge: see "Fog of war" (per-type edge spans, erasing the overlays a raised tile covers, level 0-5 parts masked to the drawn terrain).
- Borders on slopes and `terrain_to_draw`: see "Terrain art and borders". `y_delta` places only the building being placed ("Building placement").
- The sim (packages/sim): `teleport` sets every object's `z` from the ground (`TileMap.groundZ`, `tileType`; movement.ts, movement.md §4), and `missile_move` hits the ground where `rz` reaches the missile's `z` (combat.ts). The rules that read `world_z` then apply on hills: `calc_attack_modifier` (t_c_obj.cpp:288; ×1.5 for a ranged attacker above its target or past a cliff facing it, ×0.6666 for a melee one below; combat.md §2.3) and the climb/descend speed (movement.md §8.5). On the flat test map every `z` stays 0, as before. Line of sight does not read heights in the original (visible.cpp, `explore_terrain`), nor in the port.

## Fog of war (`RGE_View::view_function_terrain`, src/view.cpp:1866-2190)
The client reads `World.tileVisibility(player, col, row)` (docs/spec/visibility.md): 0x0F visible, 0x80 explored but not visible (fogged), 0 unexplored (black). Every client draws only its own player's fog, in every mode (`?debug=net`, `?debug=combat`).
- Terrain (`apps/client/src/map/fog.ts`, `map-view.ts` `setFog`): an unexplored tile is not drawn, so the canvas black shows. A fogged tile is drawn with every other pixel black: the shadowing mask (`ASMSet_Shadowing`, src/drawarea.cpp:67) has 0x00FF00FF and 0xFF00FF00 on alternate rows, so a pixel is black where `x + y` is odd in screen pixels. The edges between the three states use the tile's spans from TILEEDGE.DAT (47 `normal_draw`/`fog_draw` pairs) and BLKEDGE.DAT (47 `black_UNdraw`), loaded by `Init_Tile_Edge_Tables` (src/view.cpp:277): each file has one table per tile type 0-16 (a slope's spans cover its own box, 17, 33 or 49 rows; "Elevation" below), and a tile uses its type's (`Tile_Edge_Tables[tile->tile_type]`, src/view.cpp:2013). `EdgeNumber` (src/view.cpp:338-384) turns the 8 neighbour bits (0x80 up, 0x40 right, 0x20 down, 0x10 left, 0x08 up-right, 0x04 down-right, 0x02 down-left, 0x01 up-left) into one of the 47 edges, and `get_tile_mask_num` (src/view.cpp:2799) makes the mask. `draw_this_tile` (src/view.cpp:2012-2110): a visible tile draws its `normal_draw` plain and its `fog_draw` fogged, by the edge of its neighbours that are not visible. A fogged tile draws all of edge 0 fogged. Either kind leaves its `black_UNdraw` undrawn, by the edge of its unexplored neighbours. The pack (version 8; every tile type's tables since version 12) carries the spans as `[dy, x0, x1]` rows of the tile box (`tools/assets-import/src/map-art.ts`). The client draws the fog as an overlay layer between the terrain and the objects, an overlay per tile at its box. On a map with hills a raised tile covers part of the boxes of the tiles behind it, and the original draws the later tile's pixels, plain or fogged as that tile is, over them; the port draws the overlays in the tiles' draw order into their own texture, each tile erasing the overlays under its shape first (`map-view.ts`, an `erase` sprite per raised or sloped tile).
- Objects (`apps/client/src/units/fog-objects.ts` `fogDraw`; `RGE_Object_List::draw`, src/obj_list.cpp:117, called per tile at src/view.cpp:2170-2185): on a visible tile everything draws plain. On a fogged tile the master's `fog_flag` decides: 0 not drawn (units, and buildings too), 1 drawn fogged (mines, bushes, animals, corpses), 2 drawn fogged until it starts dying (trees), 4 not drawn where a doppelganger stands on its tile (stumps). Fogged objects use the same every-other-pixel mask, baked per screen parity (`unit-layer.ts` `foggedTexture`). On an unexplored tile the flag is not read, and only graphic parts with `draw_level` 5 or lower are queued (`SetCaptureLevel(0, 5)`, view.cpp:2174; the draw list `futur_objs` drops a node above it, `AddDrawNode` view.cpp:4317). A player's own objects follow the same rule.
- Draw levels 0-5 are clipped to the terrain (`RGE_View::draw`, view.cpp:2335-2377): after the tiles, every queued part of level 0 to 5, from any tile, is drawn twice, plain through `Terrain_Clip_Mask` (the `normal_draw` spans of the tiles drawn this frame) and fogged through `Terrain_Fog_Clip_Mask` (their `fog_draw` spans); levels 6-40 are drawn after them through the view's own mask (view.cpp:2592), plain or fogged by the node's flag. An unexplored tile adds no span to either mask (view.cpp:2111-2122), so nothing of level 0-5 shows over unexplored terrain, whatever tile the object stands on: on an unexplored tile only the pixels that hang over explored terrain show, plain or fogged as that terrain is drawn. Levels 0-5 are Gaia's terrain decorations (Terr Rock, Terr Crack, Terr Bones, Dirt, cactus), cliffs, and the shadow part of the trees (`s tree01`...) and of the Market; the tree itself is level 20. The port draws these parts plain under the fog overlay (`unit-layer.ts` `terrainZ`, a graphic with parts on both sides split in two by `composite.ts` `splitsTerrain`; `fogDraw` gives `terrain`), and the overlay covers an unexplored tile's whole diamond black (`fog.ts` `fogPixels`). That diamond overlaps only the neighbours' pixels their own `black_UNdraw` already leaves black (checked over the 8 single-neighbour edges of the pack's TILEEDGE/BLKEDGE spans), so the terrain looks as before. They are always on visible tiles, apart from corpses, which have no line of sight. Outside the map there is no terrain, so the parts of level 0-5 that hang off the map's edge (a tree's shadow on the edge tiles) are not drawn either: the port masks its `MapView.terrainLayer` (where these parts go) with the drawn terrain, the map's diamond on a flat map and every tile's surface on a map with hills.
- Buildings hidden in the fog: the Mate's decision X. AoE1 buildings have `fog_flag` 0, so an enemy building is not drawn once its tile is fogged, and nothing remembers it as last seen. The brief asked for remembered buildings, but the original does not remember them.
- Remembered resources (`Doppelgangers`, `RGE_Doppleganger_Object`): an object whose master has `create_doppleganger_on_death` leaves a copy, with the graphic, frame and facet last seen, for each player 1+ that has its tile explored but not visible. Value 2 (trees): when it starts dying (`create_doppleganger_when_dying`, src/stat_obj.cpp:1902), and whenever its tile stops being visible while it dies (`perform_doppleganger_checks`, src/dpl_obj.cpp:403). Value 1 (mines, bushes): when it is removed (`remove_visible_resource`, src/stat_obj.cpp:1866). A copy goes once its tile is visible again (`RGE_Doppleganger_Object::update`, src/dpl_obj.cpp:223). The pack carries `PackUnit.doppelganger`.
- Selection: a left click only picks objects on visible tiles (`mouse_left_up_action`, vw_main.cpp:566).

## Minimap (`RGE_Diamond_Map`, src/diam_map.cpp; mouse `RGE_Diamond_Map_View`, src/vw_dimap.cpp)
`apps/client/src/hud/minimap.ts`, geometry `minimap-geometry.ts`. `GameHud` mounts it through its `map` option.
- Shape: map.bmp, interfac.drs bina 50401 (`set_bitmap("map.bmp", 0xc4e1)`). `set_bitmap` (src/diam_map.cpp:66) reads, on each picture row, the first run of palette 0xFF as a line `[start_x, len]`. The real picture is 220x114. `set_world` (:152) maps the tiles onto the lines, ported in float32. `draw_tile` (:459) gives a tile's pixels, `draw_object` (:771) an object's point, `pick_tile` (:904) the tile under a pixel. The pack's `minimap` holds the lines.
- Terrain picture (`save_area`): `clear_image` fills the lines with palette 0 (outside them the panel art shows). `draw_explored_tiles` draws each tile once, the first time the player has it explored. The colour is the terrain's `map_med_color` on a flat tile (pack terrain `minimap` [hi, med, low]); a slope uses `map_low_color` for tile types 1, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13, 15 and `map_hi_color` for 2, 3, 6, 10, 11, 14, 16 (src/diam_map.cpp:530-557; `elevation.ts` `tileShade`); or the `map_color` of a `map_draw_level` 4 object on the tile or next to it.
- Objects (`draw_objects`, :634; `TRIBE_Diamond_Map_View::draw_object`): drawn in this order: Gaia's objects in white (0xFF); then for each player its doppelgangers (own copies only) and its unselected objects, in that player's colour (`map_color` of its colour table, pack `playerColours`); then the selection in white; then the player's own objects under attack, flashing between palette 0x56 and 0xFF every 333 ms (0x14D). `draw_visible_object` (:702) draws an object on a visible tile, or on a fogged one when its `fog_flag` is set. `map_draw_level` 1 draws a 3x3 square, 2 a plus, 3 a plus in the master's own `map_color`, and 0 nothing (pack unit `minimap` [level, colour]).
- View frame (`draw_view_rect`, :826): the main view's rectangle in white, `max_col_num` x `max_row_num` tiles of 64x32, clipped to the lines.
- Mouse: a left press on a tile centres the main view there, and so does dragging (`start_scroll_view`, `handle_scroll_view`). A right click sends the selected own units to the tile's middle (`mouse_right_up_action` with `interface_style` 2, `command_make_do`). That is a move order, as `UnitLayer.moveSelectedTo` gives it. Shift+right-click waypoints are not done (the sim has no waypoint command).
- Flare (`MASTER_OBJECT_FLARE` 112, docs/spec/combat.md): a ranged attack on a player's object creates a Flare for the target's owner, at the attacker. Its `map_draw_level` is 0 and its graphic is Blank (787), so it draws nothing on the minimap or the map. What shows is its line of sight (1): the attacker's tiles light up for the target's owner, with the attacker's dot in them, while the attacked object flashes. `e2e/real/real-fog.spec.ts` screenshots it (`fog-flare.png`).

## Menu, game over, score display, achievements
- Menu (`TribeMenuDialog`, src/dlg_menu.cpp:21-110; `hud/menu.ts`): 400 x 300 (335 with Resign, shown only while this player plays), buttons 360 x 30 from y 15, 35 apart, Cancel 15 lower; Quit Game 0x2439, Resign 0x2440, Achievements, Scenario Instructions, Game Settings, Help, About (the three without a port disabled). Quit Game and Resign each ask Yes/No (`STRING_ID_CONFIRM_QUIT_GAME` 0xc1d, `_RESIGN` 0xc1f). Resign sends `{type:'resign'}`; Quit Game resigns a player still playing, then leaves (docs/spec/turn-timing.md §9 item 6) and opens the end-of-game achievements. The dialogs' look is the game screen's info file 50006 (`hud/easy-panel.ts`).
- Game over (`TRIBE_Screen_Game::handle_game_update`, src/scr_game.cpp:1225-1265; `hud/game-over.ts`): on this player's win or loss "You are victorious!" 9004 / "You have been defeated!" 9005 centred (600 x 60, Copperplate 21) with won.wav 50320 / lost.wav 50321; 5 s later in multiplayer the whole map without fog and "Game over. Click Menu/Quit Game to exit." 9006 at the top (500 x 20, 8 down). Another player losing shows "%s resigned." 3013 (dropped: "%s disconnected." 3012) or "%s defeated." 3014 on the chat lines with game sound 6.
- Score display (`command_score`, F4 or the score button 0x1023; `hud/score-display.ts`): up to 8 lines at the view's lower right, `name: score/score`, sorted by team score, team, score, player (`scr_game_score_compare`), struck through for a player who lost, refreshed every 3 s.
- Achievements (`TribeAchievementsScreen`, src/scr_ach.cpp; `hud/achievements.ts`): info file 50057 (won) / 50058 (lost) at the end of the game, 50061 in game; the summary columns Military, Economy, Religion, Technology (each group's victory points), Survival and Wonder (yes/blank), Total; clicking a group's title opens its details (modes 1-4) with Back. The winner's mark is decal 52001 (won screen) or 52002. The Timeline button is disabled: the timeline graph needs per-interval history the sim does not keep.

## Relics and ruins
- Art: the relic (159) draws `Artifact1` graphic 11 (graphics/244) for a player and `Artifact1Gaia` 12 (graphics/245) for Gaia, 8 facings; the ruin (158) `Artifact R1` 7 (graphics/248). Nothing client-side: the sim's sprite after a change of owner (victory.md §10.1). `e2e/real/real-relics.spec.ts` screenshots both (`relics-held-host.png`, `relics-closeup.png`).
- Orders: the right click and the Move button order only selected own units of select level 4 or more (`get_selected_objects_to_command`, src/player.cpp:1648; `UnitLayer.orderedUnits`): a relic (4) walks, a ruin (3) is never ordered (victory.md §10.2).
- Notifications (`TRIBE_Game::notification`, src/tribegam.cpp:1990-2087; `hud/relics.ts` `heldNotices`), Standard game only, when a team starts or stops holding all relics (ruins), on every screen: captured plays artheld.wav (game sound 11) and shows 3028 "All Artifacts have been captured. Hold them for 2000 years to win!" (ruins 3033) to the holder and to a player that is its mutual ally with allied victory on both sides, 3029 "... You will lose if they are held for 2000 years!" (ruins 3034) to the others; lost plays artlost.wav (12) and shows 3030 "Control of the Artifacts was lost!" (ruins 3035) to everyone. A team is named once, by its first player. The texts 3056/3057 ("All Artifacts have been captured.") are for a non-Standard game, which `hold` never notifies. The sim sends no events: the client compares `World.victory.artifactHeld` / `ruinHeld` with their values at its last frame (all −1 at the start, src/tplayer.cpp:206-207).
- Countdown clocks (`TRIBE_Screen_Game::reset_clocks`, src/scr_game.cpp:3176-3260; `TRIBE_Panel_Time`, src/tpnl_tim.cpp; `HeldDisplay`): up to 10 panels of 170 x 20, 4 from the main view's right, from 4 down in steps of 20 (src/scr_game.cpp:923-929). `reset_clocks` (at the start and after each notification) gives, per player in order, a clock while its relic countdown runs ("Artifacts: %d Years", 11302) and one while its ruin countdown runs ("Ruins: %d Years", 11303), skipping a player that has a lower mutual ally with allied victory (the team shows one). The number is the countdown truncated, then rounded up to a multiple of 5; right-aligned, standard font, in the player's colour (`set_clock_type`, the score display's table) over its second colour one pixel off each diagonal. The Wonder clocks (type 4) and the time limit's (type 3) are not done; F11's game clock neither.

## Unit interpolation per step
Objects slide from the sim's previous step to the current one over `UnitLayerOptions.stepMs()`. `Lockstep.stepMs()` is `G * pace` within a turn, and one frame after a turn's last step (the next boundary waits for the next pass of the game loop, docs/spec/turn-timing.md §2). `?debug=combat` runs one step per timeout.
